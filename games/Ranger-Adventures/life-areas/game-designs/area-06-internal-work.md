### The parts practices — one arc, told through the seeds
The five parts-practices are one spiral (doc 04 §3), so their growth is given once:

**The Ranger Meets His Fear** (a scared part appears as a small creature before the storm — greet
it), **The Two-Voices Bridge** (two parts argue at a fork — let both speak), **The Curiosity
Compass** (points *toward, with curiosity*), **The Quiet Part** (a companion who goes silent under
stress, understood over chapters), **The U-Turn Gate** (a 2-second inward glance before a heated
reply).

**Grows — the only ladder this area needs (doc 04 §3):**
- **8–10 · naming.** A part gets a name and a creature-shape. Full stop — *stop at naming* (the
  doc's own flag). The lantern lights to show he paused; never how he felt.
- **10–12 · good intentions.** "Even the part that slammed the door was trying to help — how?"
  Ambivalence arrives: two parts can both be right (the Bridge).
- **12–14 · curiosity before judgement.** Greet, ask, don't fix. The Compass becomes the shared
  icon for the stance. The Quiet Part's long arc starts.
- **14–16 · patterns.** Noticing a recurring protector across chapters ("the part that goes quiet
  shows up when…"). Reflection only; **no unburdening, ever** (§8).
- **16–18 · leading, lightly.** Acting from the calm-curious place on purpose; the U-Turn before
  real replies. Aspirational, never a score.

**The cast of parts is his.** Over the years the creatures he named at 8 recur, drawn by him,
renamed by him — a private, growing menagerie. The game never names a part for him and never
suggests what a part means.

### Body Weather Vane + Down-Shift Toolkit — the regulation pair
`03-B` · bands 8–18 · seeds: 04 §5
**Loop.** Optional check-in before big moments: calm/alert/overwhelmed as sky and wind (never
red/yellow/green judgment colours). The Toolkit is the follow-up: breath, movement, rest — try one,
notice what *this* body likes.
**Grows.** 8–10 three sky-states, parent co-does the breath (co-regulation is the evidenced lever,
doc 04 §7) · 10–12 more sensation words unlock (granularity — the area's other evidenced lever) ·
12–14 catching the state *before* it's big (hunger/tired battery) · 14–18 his own toolkit ranked by
what works for him — self-knowledge as a private field guide.
**Boundary.** Noticing and describing = area 10 (perception); *interpreting and shifting state* =
here (the doc 07 §8 split).
**Flags.** No polyvagal/zones language as fact; tracked as check-in presence only.

### Felt-Sense Sketchbook — draw where it sits
`03-B` · bands 8–18 · seed: 04 §5
**Loop.** Draw the feeling's shape and place (knot, spark, fog); pick or invent the word that fits.
Dyslexia-friendly by design (drawing before words).
**Grows.** Colours/shapes only → body-map placement → blended feelings (two things true at once) →
16+ the sketchbook re-read privately: his own weather system, in his own hand.
**Flags.** Entry count only; content never surfaced by the game, never analysed, never leaves the
device. Dutch feeling-vocabulary needs the native-check pass before words ship (doc 04 §9).

### Crossing the Gate — arriving (new, tiny)
`03-B` · bands 8–18 · new — design hypothesis
**Loop.** Every session starts at the reserve gate: one breath, one body-notice ("what's the weather
in there today?"), then in. Twenty seconds, skippable, no streak.
**Why.** Gives every other practice a natural home — the check-in is *where the day starts*, not an
interruption. Also the game's only (gentle) transition ritual between screen-world and body.
**Grows.** It doesn't. That is the point. At most, at 16+, he designs his own gate ritual.
**Flags.** Design hypothesis; must never become a gate in the mechanical sense — the reserve opens
whether or not he pauses.

### After the Storm — when the game itself frustrated him (new, tiny)
`03-B` · bands 9–18 · new — design hypothesis
**Loop.** When a session visibly went hard (a staircase tumbled, a project stalled), one optional
beat as he leaves: "rough weather today. A part of you might have something to say — want to meet
it?" Yes → thirty seconds of naming with the Compass. No → nothing, ever.
**Why.** The EF games generate real frustration on hard days; this is the one place the inner-work
layer touches the rest of the game — practice on live (but small, recoverable) feeling, which is
where the doc's phenomenological approach actually bites.
**Grows.** 9–12 name it · 12–16 its good intention ("it wants you to be good at things") · 16+ he
notices without the prompt (the prompt retires — a scaffold that removes itself).
**Flags.** Design hypothesis; the trigger is the *game's* difficulty telemetry, never an inference
about his emotions (no affect-detection, doc 06 §10). Declining is a first-class outcome; the
prompt appears at most once per session, and can be turned off entirely.

## What was deliberately NOT added

Ideas considered and rejected against the hard lines — recorded so a future session doesn't re-invent
them: a "calm meter" (rates his state — barred by §9 charter) · mood tracking/journaling with
analysis (surveillance of feeling; MYRIAD caution) · streaks of reflection (manufactured compulsion
on the most private layer) · AI-coach conversations about his real feelings in any cloud shape
(barred outright) · gratitude prompts / affirmations (coercive-positivity, excluded by doc 04) ·
any parts-work content beyond notice-name-befriend (therapy line, §8).
