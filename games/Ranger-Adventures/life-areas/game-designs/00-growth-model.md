# 00 — The growth model: how one game ages 8 → 18

> **What this is.** The shared machinery every area doc in this folder uses to answer the two
> questions the seed lists left open: **how does the same game get harder for a decade** (growth),
> and **how does it stay fresh** (variation). It operationalises PLAN §3 (spiral + graduation ramp)
> and doc 06 §5/§6/§11 into levers a game card can simply name.
>
> **Bands are readiness bands, not birthdays.** Doc 01 §1.5 (gate by concreteness and scaffolding,
> not age) and doc 03 §1 (future orientation matures ~16) both forbid hard age-gating of *difficulty*.
> Ages below are design anchors; the actual gate is always demonstrated readiness (rungs, doc 06 §5)
> — except the **content** gates (death/culling 12+, reporting-a-neighbour 14+, doc 05 §8), which are
> hard gates and stay hard.
>
> **Status:** v1, 2 Jul 2026. Design hypothesis layer — the levers derive from the engine specs and
> conclusions; the band anchors are ours to tune by observation.

---

## 1. The five bands and the world that grows with them

The fiction ages with the boy. Each band opens a bigger world, a bigger role, and a new *kind* of
responsibility — so growing complexity is something he can *see* (a bigger map, a deeper title),
never a number that rates him.

| Band | Rank (fiction) | The world | What arrives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8–10** | Junior Ranger | The **Home Meadow** — one small, safe corner; the mentor close by | Everything concrete and enactive; co-play rich; one project at a time; senses and sorting |
| **10–12** | Field Ranger | The **whole reserve** opens | First own budget; first multi-step projects; the mentor starts fading (doc 03 Help-Fade); calibration gets numbers |
| **12–14** | Ranger | The **neighbours** arrive — farms, village, visitors | Stakeholders and real dilemmas (age-gate lifts); the workshop (making); Engine-04 characters *if* PLAN §7.6 lands; validity-vs-truth reasoning |
| **14–16** | Head Ranger | The **region** — grants, ventures, other reserves | Parallel projects under a WIP cap; the in-fiction **assistant** to direct and verify (the delegate-&-check curriculum); real-money lane opens (doc 02 §7) |
| **16–18** | Warden-in-training | The **real world** — the game inverts | Real projects/tools are the quest; the game becomes the reflection + almanac layer (graduation ramp, doc 06 §11); at 18 the Almanac is a real record of a decade |

**The Almanac is the spine.** Area 4's Reserve Almanac (the before/after book that *is* the reward)
generalises into the whole game's portfolio: every band closes as a *chapter* — projects finished,
things made, dilemmas faced, species learned. At 18 it is a genuine artefact of a childhood, and the
graduation gift. (Model B made visible; never a score — doc 06 §9.)

**The cast ages too.** Recurring characters (the farmer, the farmer's kid, the mentor, the village)
grow older along with the ranger. Relationships deepen band over band, which lets communication and
ethics content mature *inside the fiction* instead of via a difficulty slider.

## 3. The authorship flip — the AI-era through-line

The one arc that runs through *every* area, and the sharpest translation of the PLAN §1 thesis into
game design: **at 8 the game asks him questions; at 17 he writes the briefs and checks the answers.**

| Stage | The child is… | Example (any area) |
|---|---|---|
| answer | responding to given items | "which call is this?" |
| judge | evaluating others' / AI's work | Critique Cards, Spot the AI Slop |
| produce | making the thing himself | scope a project, draft the poster |
| **specify & verify** | writing the brief, delegating, checking the result | Delegate or Do?, Art Director, "asking machines" |
| teach / curate | explaining it, choosing what matters | Teach the New Volunteer, The Gallery, self-chosen fluency decks |

When generation is cheap, the durable human position is the *asking, specifying and verifying* side
of the loop. The late bands of nearly every card land there deliberately. (This is design stance, not
a research claim — the transfer honesty table, doc 06 §8, still binds.)

## 4. Session cadence (where spaced rehearsal hides)

Growing complexity is also *rhythm*: the game runs on four loops, and the resurfacing scheduler
(doc 06 §6) plants its triggers inside them rather than nagging.

- **Daily-ish field drill** (5 min) — one Model-A skin at staircase level (calls, percents, facts).
  Optional, forgiving; a gentle map, never a breakable chain (motivation rulebook).
- **Weekly reserve walk** (10–15 min) — the doc-03 review: pass all live projects, re-scope, tick
  done-states, one calibration check ("done by Friday?" resolved).
- **Seasonal expedition** (the big unit) — a multi-area quest ([11-expeditions.md](11-expeditions.md));
  this is where a rusty rung resurfaces *inside a fresh story* (interleaving trigger, doc 06 §6).
- **Yearly Big Count + chapter close** — the almanac chapter, the phenology wheel completing a ring,
  the rank check-in. The spiral made visible: same events every year, one rung deeper.

## 5. Variation systems (staying fresh without new code)

1. **Seasonal stimulus refresh** — the same skin, new data: spring dawn chorus vs winter calls,
   summer tracks vs snow prints. A stimulus library tagged by season *is* the content calendar.
2. **Weather & time-of-day modifiers** — rain muffles calls (noise lever), dusk shortens exposure,
   snow makes tracks easy but ages them fast. Difficulty rises *in the fiction* (engine 01 §6).
3. **Domain swaps** — every engine's `library_ref` knob: the same calibration loop over tracks,
   weather, effort estimates; the same critique loop over posters, trail layouts, AI drafts.
4. **The cast** (§1) — new personas are data records (engine 04 §4); the *same* NVC move replays
   against a new person carrying something new.
5. **Expedition recurrence** — the same expedition returns a year later one rung deeper (the pond
   needs maintenance; the count needs a sampling plan). Spiral, playable.
6. **Co-play toggles** — many cards have a parent-present variant (co-regulation lever, doc 04 §7);
   flipping solo↔co-play is itself variety.

## 6. The graduation ramp, per band (doc 06 §11 applied)

- **8–12:** everything is sim or hybrid; the parent is co-player, not grader.
- **12–14:** first *hybrid-real* lanes — a real saving goal, a real small project on a simplified
  board, a real sit-spot. The game logs and debriefs; reality does the teaching.
- **14–16:** real-money lane (co-managed index fund, doc 02 §7); real making with real audiences;
  the game's job shifts to *scaffolding + reflection* for those lanes.
- **16–18:** per-area handoff on the doc-06 §11 signals (rung 3 stable, wants it real, low-risk
  opportunity, parent can debrief without grading). The dependency trap check is live: if the real
  thing only happens with the game open, force the graduation.
- **The Almanac never graduates** — reflection and the record are the one layer that stays.
