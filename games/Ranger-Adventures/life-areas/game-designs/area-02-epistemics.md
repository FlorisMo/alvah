### Sure-O-Meter — the calibration flagship, and a layer over everything
`engine 02` · `Model A` · bands 8–18 · seed: 01 §5–§6
**Loop.** Answer, say how sure, see the honest reveal; over many trials a personal calibration curve
builds. Honesty meter separate from knowledge meter — being well-calibrated is a win even when wrong.
**Grows (L1, L5).** 8–9 three faces + the confidence jar ("was the jar right?") · 9–12 five stars,
curve shown as filled/empty jars per star band · 12–14 probability slider (50–100%), Brier vs the
0.25 always-50% baseline, named gently ("beat the coin-flipper") · 14–16 full 0–100%, **interval
estimates** ("the pond is between … and … litres" — Guess-the-Forest seam) · 16–18 calibration
*across domains* compared: where am I over/under-confident — tracks? people? time estimates?
**The layer move (the big optimization).** From ~12 the Sure-O-Meter stops being only a game and
becomes a **service any other skin can invoke** — any verdict anywhere ("which call?", "done by
Friday?", "is this claim solid?") can carry the same elicitation and feed the same curve. One
calibration identity across the whole game; engine 02 already composes this way.
**Varies.** Item domains as data: field claims, weather, effort estimates (area 04's
Predict-Then-Check *is* this reskinned — doc 03 §5), history-of-the-reserve claims.
**Flags.** Watch over-correction into under-confidence (doc 06 §8); feedback is a map, never a
grade; expect childhood overconfidence as the visible, shrinkable baseline (doc 01 §6).

### Prediction Diary — dated beliefs, resolved honestly
`engine 02 (delayed reveal)` · `Model A` · bands 10–18 · seed: 01 §5
**Loop.** Write (speak) a dated prediction; the game resurfaces it at resolution time; Brier over
months, shown as a growing track record.
**Grows (L5, L9).** 10–12 tomorrow/this-week predictions (weather, the pond, the football score) ·
12–14 season-scale (first frost, migration return — phenology seam) · 14–16 real-life predictions
he chooses, private by default · 16–18 a yearly "forecast review" ritual: which *kinds* of
predictions go wrong, and why (the superforecasting habit, sized for one person).
**Varies.** Reserve almanac predictions; family-scale ("will the tomatoes beat the slugs?");
paired-with-parent rival forecasts (co-play, friendly).
**Flags.** Forecasting habits are *zeker* in adults, untested in kids (doc 01 §4) — keep it a diary
with a curve, not a tournament.

### The Mind-Changer — "what would change my mind?"
`engine 02 + 03` · `A+B` · bands 10–18 · new — design hypothesis
**Loop.** Before the reveal, the child states not just a belief but **the evidence that would flip
it**. Then evidence arrives (sometimes the flipping kind). Updating on your own named trigger feels
like winning, not losing — the game's frame: *changing your mind on evidence is the ranger's
sharpest skill.*
**Grows (L4, L10).** 10–12 pick the would-change-my-mind item from three options · 12–14 name it
yourself; the game hunts for it · 14–16 design the *test* that would settle it (CVS seam) · 16–18
apply it socially: in a disagreement, ask the other side "what would change yours?" (Engine 04, if
unblocked).
**Varies.** Field mysteries, dilemma pre-commitments (ethics seam: "what fact would change my
Intervene Dial setting?").
**Flags.** Design hypothesis, built directly on the doc 01 §3 rung ("what would change my mind" is
named in the area's what-it-is but had no seed). The falsification frame stays playful, never
gotcha.

### Source Sleuth — who is behind this, and how would I check?
`engine 03` · `Model B (judgement-of-meaning)` · bands 8–18 · seed: 01 §5
**Loop.** Before judging a claim, leave the claim: check who made it, what others say about them,
whether better sources exist (the lateral-reading move, the area's evidenced backbone).
**Grows (L4, L9, L10).** 8–10 source *tagging* — saw it / was told / worked it out (doc 01 §3) ·
10–12 pick who's behind a claim from two profiles; notice "too clean" claims · 12–14 mock-web
lateral reading: open tabs, find who funds the "Save Our Heath" site · 14–16 the real web, parent
nearby: SIFT moves on live topics he cares about · 16–18 **evaluate an AI answer end-to-end** —
trace its claims to sources, mark what checks out (experimental label, doc 01 §7).
**Varies.** In-fiction newsletter and notice-board claims; visitor rumours; two NPC "experts" with
different incentives (the hunting-shop owner on wolf numbers).
**Flags.** Lateral reading is *zeker* for web sources; transfer to AI output is *waarschijnlijk,
unproven* — say so in the design, keep the web version the spine.

### Trust the Photo? — provenance thinking for the image era
`engine 03` · `Model B` · bands 12–18 · new — design hypothesis (experimental)
**Loop.** A photo "proves" something (a wolf at the pond, a giant pike). The skill is **not**
eye-spotting fakes — it is choosing the *verification move*: reverse-search thinking, asking for the
original, checking date/place, finding a second source.
**The honest twist (this is the whole design).** Even trained adults detect manipulation poorly
(doc 01 §7's ~55% line for hallucinations generalises the lesson) — so the game *teaches that eyes
alone cannot settle it*. Rounds where "inspect harder" is a trap and "check provenance" wins are the
curriculum.
**Grows (L2, L10).** 12–14 pick the best checking move from options; obvious fakes mixed with real
oddities (nature is weirder than fakes) · 14–16 run the checks on a mock feed; write the one-line
verdict with confidence (Sure-O-Meter layer) · 16–18 live topics with parent; and the flip side —
*his own* photos get a provenance note ("taken by me, date, place"), building the habit from the
producer side.
**Varies.** Audio too ("is this recording really a wolf?"); quotes ("did the mayor really say
this?").
**Flags.** Experimental — no efficacy evidence for detection training; the defensible skill is the
checking *process* (lateral-reading lineage), never "I can spot fakes."

### Spot the Confident Robot — the hallucinating field assistant
`engine 02 (calibration half) + 05 (critique half)` · `A+B` · bands 10–18 · seed: 01 §5 (experimental)
**Loop.** A scripted in-game "AI assistant" answers field questions fluently — and sometimes
confidently wrong. Flag the suspicious claim, pick the check, then the reveal.
**Grows (L2, L10).** 10–12 wrongness is blatant (wrong bird in the picture) · 12–14 subtle-but-
checkable (plausible wrong date, invented "fact" with a real-sounding name) · 14–16 the assistant
*cites sources* — some invented; verify citations (Source Sleuth seam) · 16–18 he *uses* the
assistant for real work and the verification habit is the graded-by-life skill: what did you check
before relying on it? (Delegate-or-Do seam, area 04.)
**Varies.** The robot's error *types* rotate (wrong entity, outdated, overgeneralised, fabricated
source) — a field guide of failure modes builds in the almanac.
**Flags.** Flagged **experimental** in doc 01 §1.4/§7 (essentially no efficacy evidence; adults
detect ~55%). Fully scripted/local — needs no cloud (doc 01 §10). The lesson is always "how would I
check", never "I can smell lies."

### When Experts Disagree — map the disagreement
`engine 03` · `Model B` · bands 14–18 · new — design hypothesis
**Loop.** Two credible sources genuinely conflict (the Oostvaardersplassen framings, doc 05 §7, are
the template). The skill: locate *where* they split — facts, values, definitions, or predictions —
and produce a one-page "state of the disagreement" that both would sign.
**Grows (L4, L10).** 14–16 given both texts, sort their claims into the four split-types · 16–18
build the map from raw sources, then state his own provisional stance *and* what evidence would
move it (Mind-Changer seam).
**Varies.** Conservation debates, nutrition headlines, AI-risk takes (meta!), local-news rows.
**Flags.** Design hypothesis; leans on the dilemma-discussion evidence (doc 05 §4, P4C) and the
steelman move. The game never announces a winner — *experts themselves disagree* is the truth being
taught (doc 05 §9).

### Steelman First — the strongest version of the other side
`engine 03` · `Model B (rubric: did the other side feel understood?)` · bands 11–18 · seed: 01 §5
**Loop.** Before disagreeing, state the other view so well its holder says "yes, that's it." Only
then argue.
**Grows (L8).** 11–13 pick the fairest restatement from three · 13–15 produce it, spoken; an NPC
confirms or corrects (Engine 04 when unblocked — the "feel understood" check is what generative
reaction is *for*) · 15–18 steelman positions he finds genuinely wrong (the hard mode), then in real
disagreements, debriefed privately.
**Varies.** Shared muscle, three homes: epistemics (claims), ethics (Steel-Man the Other Ranger,
area 08), communication (Steelman the Stranger, area 05) — one engine record, three data skins.
**Flags.** *Waarschijnlijk* via examples (doc 01 §4); pairs with perspective-taking's development
arc (doc 04 §3 — recursive ToM matures late; don't demand it early).
